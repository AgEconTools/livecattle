        How this is calculated

        Weights are USDA's weighted averages for the same cattle as the prices: live weight for live prices, carcass (dressed) weight for dressed prices.

                How this is calculated

                The cutout is USDA's estimate of what a carcass's beef brings when sold in boxes to retailers and restaurants: the value of its cuts, in dollars per hundredweight of carcass. These are USDA's National Daily Boxed Beef Cutout figures (afternoon report) for Choice and Select carcasses of 600-900 lb, from negotiated sales packers report under Livestock Mandatory Reporting. A load is about 40,000 lb of boxed beef. A primal is one of the carcass's eight major parts (rib, chuck, round, loin, brisket, short plate, flank); USDA values each in $/cwt of that primal, so a primal's value isn't a share of the cutout's. Weekly and monthly values are the simple average of the period's daily values; the this-year chart uses finished weeks only, and the latest month since 2010 can be partial. The Choice-Select spread is the Choice value minus the Select value, for the cutout or the primal picked.

                About these numbers

                Fed cattle and boxed beef prices come from USDA Agricultural Marketing Service Livestock Mandatory Reporting, which packers file for their cattle purchases and boxed beef sales.

                How these numbers are calculated
                • Fed cattle prices are USDA's weighted averages of negotiated cash purchases (a price agreed that week, for delivery within 14 days), for steers and heifers of every grade together unless a Choice grade band is named. Formula, forward contract and negotiated grid purchases are not included. Live prices are per hundredweight of live weight at the feedlot (FOB); dressed prices are per hundredweight of carcass weight, delivered to the packing plant.
                • The regions are USDA's. 5-Area covers feedlots in Texas-Oklahoma-New Mexico, Kansas, Nebraska, Colorado and Iowa-Minnesota; National covers every reporting U.S. feedlot (domestic cattle); South Central covers Colorado, Kansas and Texas-Oklahoma-New Mexico together, since Nov. 1, 2026. USDA withholds a region's figures for a week when too few cattle trade to keep them confidential.
                • A week is labeled by its Monday. Monthly prices average the month's weeks by head.
                • How cattle were bought (This week's About card) is USDA's national weekly report of cattle delivered to packers by purchase type: negotiated cash, negotiated grid (a price negotiated for the base, then the packer's grid of premiums and discounts by carcass), formula (priced from another market, such as a cash average or futures, plus the grid) and forward contract (priced before the cattle are ready, often from futures). Shares count every animal delivered, domestic and imported, as USDA's published negotiated share does.
                • Negotiated cash trade isn't priced by grade, only by the share of a pen expected to grade Choice, so USDA reports no cash price for Select cattle.
                • Boxed beef is USDA's National Daily Boxed Beef Cutout (afternoon report), Choice and Select, 600-900 lb carcasses, negotiated sales. It is national: the region picked at the top doesn't change it.
